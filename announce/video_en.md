> Last week Silicon Valley went crazy over a model called Jev.
> It doesn't chat. It doesn't write code. It only makes decisions.
>
> I can't program. But I had a hunch: what if it's just a fast text encoder
> with a tiny decision head on top?
>
> So I handed that hunch to Claude Code and told it to find out.
> Ten thousand decisions. Four public datasets. CPU only, on a laptop.
>
> Result: the open stack scored seventy-eight point seven.
> Jev scored seventy-nine point three. **Six tenths of a point apart.**
>
> But here's the part that got me. An eight-million-parameter static embedding,
> plus a decision layer that is literally **eight floating point numbers** —
> zero point one milliseconds. Eighty-two megabytes. Trains in a tenth of a second.
>
> All open source. Including the eight ideas that failed.
>
> My only real job was telling the AI when it had gone off track.
